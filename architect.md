AI Agent Skill: Pop Documentary Architect
Objective: Transform a niche topic into a cinematic, high-retention human-interest story using the "Experience before Understanding" philosophy.

Phase 1: The Concept & The "Promise"
Human Interest Core: Identify the human element. Documentaries must move beyond purely educational goals to focus on "The Human Condition"—emotions, achievements, or problems that resonate universally.

The Promise: Define the "Title/Thumbnail" first. The script must be designed specifically to fulfill the curiosity sparked by the thumbnail (the "Payoff") while simultaneously broadening the viewer’s interest into deeper topics.

Phase 2: Narrative Engineering (The 3-Act Arc)
Structure the script using a classic narrative framework to maintain tension :

Act 1 (The Setup): Identify the "Hero" (person, place, or idea) and the "Incisive Moment" that creates a goal or problem.

Act 2 (The Conflict): Compound the problems. Introduce external obstacles (systems, nature) or internal ones (self-doubt). The tension must peak here.

Act 3 (The Resolution): Provide a conclusion where the goal is reached or lost, and the subject undergoes a transformation.

Phase 3: The "Anchor-Bridge" Hook Strategy
The Visual Anchor: Start the script with a compelling "Visual Anchor"—a vivid detail or object that triggers curiosity. Use the "Look at this thing!" prompt.

The Curiosity Gap: Intentionally withhold context initially. Create a mystery (the gap) between what the viewer sees and what they understand.

The Contextual Bridge: Only after the audience is visually "hungry" for answers should you provide the "Contextual Bridge" (the history or facts) to explain the anchor.

Phase 4: Two-Column A/V Scripting
The agent must generate the script in a granular two-column format to ensure audio and visuals are choreographed in unison :

Left Column (Audio): Spoken prose, broken down into 1–2 sentence rows. Use an "Explainer" voice for clarity and a "Poetic" voice to build empathy.

Right Column (Visuals): Precise visual choreography. Identify "Evidence" (visuals that prove facts) vs. "Soul Moments" (visuals used purely for mood/vibe).

Tagging System: Use color codes or tags: **** for needed assets (archival, maps), [Green] for fact-checked statements with citations, and **** for directing cues.

Phase 5: The Auditory & Aesthetic Layer ("Art Ingredients")
Sound Design: Plan for a 3D auditory landscape. Specify Foley (rustle of leaves, footsteps) and "Thematic Motifs" (recurring sounds like a ticking clock) to reinforce emotional beats.

Visual Bible: Establish "Art Ingredients"—specific instructions for archival treatment, 3D modeling (low-poly/glow bloom look), and lighting (side-lighting for depth).

Phase 6: Strategic CTA Integration
Avoid generic requests. Use a three-tier placement strategy :

Early (10% in): An "Ultra-light" promise tied to value (e.g., "Watch to the end to see how this pattern breaks").

Middle (Value Peak): A provocative question in the script to foster community discourse.

End (Resolution): A "Join the family/mission" prompt followed by a "Next Video" recommendation to extend session time.
